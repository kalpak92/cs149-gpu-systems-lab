# Source reconciliation

## Evidence layers

Keep these layers distinct:

| Layer | Establishes | Does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Video metadata | Source identity, duration, publication context | What was said throughout the video |
| Transcript | Approximate spoken sequence and searchable timestamps | Exact wording when captions are automatic |
| Slides | Displayed definitions, diagrams, and numerical examples | Every qualification or correction spoken aloud |
| Existing notes | Prior organization and learner context | Accuracy against the current primary sources |
| Interpretation | A reasoned explanation or connection | A directly observed lecture statement |
| Learner reflection | The learner's understanding and voice | A conclusion Codex should invent |

## Reconciliation rules

1. Prefer the referenced offering's video and slides over a different year's
   material. Use another offering only as labeled supporting context.
2. Use the transcript for chronology and spoken emphasis. Verify terminology,
   equations, and precise numbers against slides or another primary source.
3. Rewatch the exact video interval before presenting automatic-caption text as
   a quotation. Otherwise paraphrase and label uncertainty.
4. Preserve corrections, false starts, and source disagreements when they
   materially affect meaning. Do not silently smooth them away.
5. If the video ends while the slide deck continues, identify which claims are
   video-supported and which come from the remaining slides.
6. Organize detailed notes conceptually only after creating a chronological
   topic map. Retain timestamp ranges near source-sensitive claims.
7. Do not turn every aside or classroom question into a permanent note. Include
   it when it clarifies the mental model, corrects a misconception, defines a
   boundary, or changes the interpretation.

## Full-review standard

Call a transcript fully reviewed only when all are true:

- the first transcript timestamp is close to the video start;
- the last timestamp is close to the video duration;
- no unexplained internal timestamp gap exceeds the chosen threshold;
- every generated chronological ledger interval was read and marked complete;
- the beginning, middle, and end were sampled against the raw transcript after
  normalization;
- slide-only or inferred material is labeled separately.

Downloading the whole file, inspecting selected timestamps, or searching every
named topic does not meet this standard.

## Note-editing checklist

- Lead with the lecture's central question or mental model.
- Put a compact high-level map before detailed sections when it improves
  comprehension.
- Preserve the lecture's causal progression, especially when later mechanisms
  answer problems introduced earlier.
- Explain illustrative hardware numbers as observations, not universal
  requirements.
- Qualify peak-capability calculations as upper bounds, not performance claims.
- Preserve explicit pending sections for learner understanding, confusion, and
  infrastructure connection.
